# Practical neural-network-based alternatives to epiplexity for measuring learnable structure (dataset- and example-level)

Scope note: this file covers practical estimators: prequential/online MDL, learning-curve measures, example-level difficulty and learnability scores, compression-based measures, intrinsic dimension and weight-information measures, and V-information/PVI. Their relation to epiplexity (Finzi, Qiu, Jiang, Izmailov, Kolter, Wilson, arXiv:2601.03220) is covered throughout. Formulas were checked against the PDFs and HTML of the primary sources (retrieved 2026-09-23). Bibliographic venue and page data come from arXiv metadata and the Semantic Scholar API. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** were not confirmed against a primary source in this pass.

## Q0. Method catalog: exact score, canonical reference, granularity, required training runs, compute, relation to epiplexity

### Takeaway
Epiplexity's cheap "prequential estimate" is the area of the online loss curve above the final loss. It is essentially the same quantity as (a) the "information the trained parameters contain about the data" that Blier & Ollivier (2018) read off prequential codes, (b) the "model cost" of Voita & Titov (2020), and (c) most closely, the held-out "information transfer" $L_{IT}$ of Zhang et al. (2020). The epiplexity paper explicitly adopts Zhang et al.'s heuristic. Most other popular scores fall into two groups:
- Total-codelength and difficulty scores (online codelength, EL2N, forgetting, VoG, intrinsic dimension on train accuracy, gzip/LLM compression). These rise for noise.
- "Learnability" scores (RHO-LOSS, JEST). These are example-level, model-state-relative analogues of "area above the floor", and are the closest example-level cousins of epiplexity.

### Cited Findings

#### 0.A Epiplexity itself (reference point)
- Title/authors: "From Entropy to Epiplexity: Rethinking Information for Computationally Bounded Intelligence", Marc Finzi, Shikai Qiu, Yiding Jiang, Pavel Izmailov, J. Zico Kolter, Andrew Gordon Wilson. arXiv:2601.03220 (v2 dated 16 Mar 2026). Code: github.com/shikaiqiu/epiplexity — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Definition: $P^\star=\arg\min_{P\in\mathcal P_T}\{|P|+\mathbb E[\log 1/P(X)]\}$, $S_T(X):=|P^\star|$ (epiplexity), $H_T(X):=\mathbb E[\log 1/P^\star(X)]$ (time-bounded entropy). $\mathcal P_T$ is the set of prefix-free programs that sample and evaluate in time $T(n)$ — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Prequential estimate (their Eq. 8): $|P_{\rm preq}|\approx\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\left(\log 1/P_i(Z_i)-\log 1/P_M(Z_i)\right)$, "the area under the loss curve above the final loss". It is derived as $L(Z_{:M},P_M)-L(Z_{:M}\mid P_M)$ via symmetry of information, "adopt[ing] the heuristic in Zhang et al. (2020) and Finzi et al. (2025)". Time bound: $6ND+2N\mathcal D$ FLOPs — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Paper's own caveats on the prequential estimate: it is "not rigorous for two reasons". (1) Both code lengths only upper-bound Kolmogorov complexities, so their difference does not bound $K(P_M)$. (2) Symmetry of information does not extend to time-bounded K. The paper also warns that prequential losses "are effectively taken on estimates of the test loss… In cases where train and test diverge, such as when there is overfitting, this difference could become important." — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Behaviour on noise and on trivial data: "For random data, $\log 1/P_i(Z_i)$ never decreases, while for simple data, $\log 1/P_i(Z_i)$ drops rapidly and stabilizes, both leading to small $|P_{\rm preq}|$." — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Requential estimate (Eq. 9): $|P_{\rm req}|=\sum_{i}\mathrm{KL}(P^t_i\|P^s_i)+\log(1+\mathrm{KL}(P^t_i\|P^s_i))+4+O(1)$. The student trains on samples from the teacher, and the samples are sent with relative-entropy coding. The two estimates correlate well, especially within dataset groups, and the prequential estimate is typically larger — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Practical protocol: μP and CompleteP learning-rate transfer. EMA of iterates under a constant LR schedule "rather than using a decaying learning rate schedule, following Hägele et al. (2024)", so one run gives all token budgets. Sweeps over depth and width. The Pareto frontier is estimated with a lower convex hull, because the empirical frontier "is jagged and includes many spurious points". For a power-law loss curve $L=E+(D/D_0)^{-\beta}$ there is a closed form $|P_{\rm preq}|=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}D_0\,d^{1-\beta}$ (Eq. 54) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- The related-work section of 2601.03220 cites Whitney et al. (2020, SDL), Zhang et al. (2020, information transfer), Xu et al. (2020, V-entropy), excess entropy (Crutchfield & Packard; Feldman 1998), Dziugaite & Roy (2025), Hutter (2021) and ADO (Jiang et al. 2025). Text search of the v2 HTML found **no** mention of Blier & Ollivier, Voita & Titov, Bornschein, Mindermann (RHO-LOSS), Ethayarajh (PVI), Toneva, Swayamdipta, EL2N, Perez (RDA) or Yogatama. The review should draw these connections itself — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- On V-entropy: the paper says pseudoentropy and V-entropy "capture only the random component of information since [they] still measure the unpredictability… under the best feasible model", and that V-entropy's constraint "only limits the inference time, and does not account for the time to find such a model" — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- On SDL: "Whitney et al. (2020) introduced surplus description length (SDL), which is the summed online loss of the training algorithm, with either the entropy of the data or a fixed baseline performance subtracted out… smaller SDL is preferred [for representations]… In contrast, we seek to… increase epiplexity." — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)

#### 0.B 2026 follow-ups by the epiplexity group (directly relevant to practical estimation)
- Requential coding paper: Shikai Qiu, Marc Finzi, Yujia Zheng, Kun Zhang, Andrew Gordon Wilson, "Requential Coding: Pushing the Limits of Model Compression with Self-Generated Training Data", arXiv:2607.11883 (13 Jul 2026). Code: github.com/shikaiqiu/requential-coding — [arXiv:2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11883)
- Its decomposition of why raw prequential code length is not a structure measure: $\mathbb E[L_{\rm preq}(X_{0:T-1})]=\sum_t H(X_t)+\sum_t\mathbb E[\mathrm{KL}(P^\star\|P_t)]$. The data-entropy term accumulates "at a linear rate even after the model stops learning". The requential code length "is independent of parameter count and data entropy". It "isolates the learnable information in a dataset from its unpredictable, random content" — [arXiv HTML 2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11883v1)
- Requential compute: FLOPs ratio of roughly 1/3 : 1 : 1 (sampling : student step : teacher step). The overhead drops to about 0.33× if teacher checkpoints already exist. ORC encoding draws about $2^{\mathrm{KL}}$ proposals per call, so encoding can be slow; splitting batches into blocks trades code length for encoding time. Stabilizers: EMA of teacher and student, "teacher smoothing", "iso-loss projection" — [arXiv HTML 2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11883v1)
- EpiSelect/EpiGen paper: Ellen Su, Andres Potapczynski, Shikai Qiu, Edward Hughes, Andrew Gordon Wilson, "Epiplexity Guided Data Selection and Generation for Out-of-Distribution Generalization", arXiv:2608.11746 (12 Aug 2026). EpiSelect fits per-domain scaling laws to training-loss curves to predict "expected epiplexity gain" and set domain sampling weights. EpiGen uses the reward $r_t=S(t)-S(t-1)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal B}\left(\log\frac{1}{P_{\theta_{t-1}}(x)}-\log\frac{1}{P_{\theta_t}(x)}\right)$ on a buffer of generated data, optimized with REINFORCE. Rationale: "as both trivial and noisy data have near-zero epiplexity, this objective creates data that challenges the learner while remaining predictable." — [arXiv HTML 2608.11746](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11746)

#### 0.C Prequential / online-code MDL family
- **Blier & Ollivier (2018)**, "The Description Length of Deep Learning Models", NeurIPS 2018 (Advances in NeurIPS 31; pages 2220–2230 per Semantic Scholar), arXiv:1802.07044. Definition 3: $L^{\rm preq}(y_{1:n}|x_{1:n})=t_1\log_2K+\sum_{s=0}^{S-1}-\log_2 p_{\hat\theta_{t_s}}(y_{t_s+1:t_{s+1}}|x_{t_s+1:t_{s+1}})$, where $\hat\theta_{t_s}$ is trained on samples $1..t_s$. Level: dataset. Runs: one training per block, with a stopping criterion (they used dropout plus early stopping) — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- Blier & Ollivier already interpret the epiplexity-style difference: "The difference between the prequential codelength $L^{\rm preq}(y_{1:n}|x_{1:n})$ and the log-loss $\sum_{t=1}^n-\log_2 p_{\hat\theta_{t_K}}(y_t|x_t)$ of the final trained model, can be interpreted as the amount of information that the trained parameters contain about the data" — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- Blier & Ollivier numbers: MNIST uniform code 199 kbits (60000·log2 10), best prequential code 4.10 kbits (ratio 0.02, VGG-like). CIFAR-10 uniform 166 kbits, prequential 45.3 kbits (ratio 0.27, VGGb), switch code 34.6 kbits (ratio 0.21). Blocks: MNIST $t_s=8,16,\dots,32768$; CIFAR $10,20,\dots,40960$. Optimizer: Adam, lr 1e-3. The CIFAR runs include "a small MLP" with two hidden layers of 512 units — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- **Voita & Titov (2020)**, "Information-Theoretic Probing with Minimum Description Length", EMNLP 2020, pp. 183–196, doi:10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.14, arXiv:2003.12298. Online code: $L^{\rm online}(y_{1:n}|x_{1:n})=t_1\log_2K-\sum_{i=1}^{S-1}\log_2p_{\theta_i}(y_{t_i+1:t_{i+1}}|x_{t_i+1:t_{i+1}})$. Timesteps: 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, 100% of data. They compare against the uniform code $n\log_2K$ and report "model cost" as online codelength minus the data codelength under the model trained on all data. "The online code is related to the area under the learning curve." Code: github.com/lena-voita/description-length-probing — [arXiv:2003.12298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)
- **Yogatama et al. (2019)**, "Learning and Evaluating General Linguistic Intelligence", arXiv:1901.11373. Online codelength $\ell(A)=\log_2|Y|-\sum_{i=2}^N\log_2 p(y_i|x_i;\hat W_{A_{i-1}})$ (block version $|S_1|\log_2|Y|-\sum_{i=2}^M\log_2 p(y_{S_i}|x_{S_i};\hat W_{S_{i-1}})$). They state it is "related to area under the learning curve (Guyon et al., 2011)" — [arXiv:1901.11373](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11373)
- **Zhang, Li, Dou, Wu (2020)**, "Measuring Information Transfer in Neural Networks", arXiv:2009.07624 (preprint; Semantic Scholar lists no venue). $L^k_{IT}(M)=L^{\rm preq}_{\theta_0}(y_{1:k}|x_{1:k})-L^{\rm preq}_M(y_{1:k}|x_{1:k})$, computed on $k$ examples **not overlapping the training set**. They argue the Blier-style train-set difference "measures both generalizable information and the noise simply remembered by the model". On CIFAR-10 with random labels, $L_{IT}$ "remains close to zero for any n" although train accuracy is high (their Fig. 10). Level: dataset/model. The epiplexity paper adopts this heuristic — [arXiv:2009.07624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07624)
- **Bornschein, Visin, Osindero (2020)**, "Small Data, Big Decisions: Model Selection in the Small-Data Regime", ICML 2020, PMLR 119:1035–1044, arXiv:2009.12583 — [arXiv:2009.12583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12583); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/bornschein20a.html)
- **Bornschein, Li, Hutter (2022/2023)**, "Sequential Learning of Neural Networks for Prequential MDL", arXiv:2210.07931 (ICLR 2023 per Semantic Scholar venue). Block-wise approximation: $\sum_{i=1}^N\log p(y_i|\hat\theta(D_{<i}))\approx\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\sum_{j=s_k}^{s_{k+1}}\log p(y_j|\hat\theta(D_{<s_k}))$. They compare chunk-incremental from scratch (CI/FS), chunk-incremental fine-tuning (CI/CF), and mini-batch incremental with a replay buffer or replay streams (MI/RB, MI/RS). "online-learning with rehearsal has favorable performance compared to the previously widely used block-wise estimation" — [arXiv:2210.07931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Bornschein et al. 2022 results (MI/RS, cumulative loss in **nats**): MNIST LeNet 4.4k (1.4e13 FLOPs); MNIST MnistNet 2.1k (4.5e14 FLOPs); CIFAR-10 VGG++ 22.1k (5.9e15 FLOPs); CIFAR-10 WRN-28-10 22.9k (3.7e16 FLOPs). For comparison, Blier & Ollivier's CIFAR-10 result converts to 31.4k nats — [arXiv:2210.07931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- **Bornschein, Chiappa, Malek, Ke (2021)**, "Prequential MDL for Causal Structure Learning with Neural Networks", arXiv:2107.05481 — [arXiv:2107.05481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05481)
- **Perez, Kiela, Cho (2021)**, "Rissanen Data Analysis: Examining Dataset Characteristics via Description Length", ICML 2021 (PMLR 139; pages [UNVERIFIED]), arXiv:2103.03872. $L_p(y_{1:N}|x_{1:N})=\sum_{n=1}^N-\log_2 p_{\theta_n}(y_n|x_n)$ with block approximation. RDA compares $\bar L_p(y|x,f)$ against $\bar L_p(y|x)$ to test whether a capability or feature $f$ helps. Setup: $S=9$ blocks, $t_1=64$, log-uniform spacing. Each block uses a 90/10 train/dev split for hyperparameters and early stopping, and uses $H(y)$ as a baseline. They report mean ± std. error over 5 seeds, sweeping hyperparameters only on the first seed, and train an ensemble over model classes. Code: github.com/ethanjperez/rda — [arXiv:2103.03872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03872)
- **Whitney, Song, Brandfonbrener, Altosaar, Cho (2020)**, "Evaluating representations by the complexity of learning low-loss predictors", arXiv:2009.07368 (preprint). Definition 2 (SDL): $m_{\rm SDL}(\phi,D,\mathcal A,\varepsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^\infty[L(\mathcal A_\phi,i)-\varepsilon]_+$ with $\varepsilon\ge H(Y|X)$. Definition 3 (εSC): $m_{\varepsilon SC}=\min\{n: L(\mathcal A_\phi,n)\le\varepsilon\}$. Here $L(\mathcal A_\phi,n)$ is the expected population loss after training on $n$ points. MDL and validation accuracy are "slices and integrals along the data axis of the loss-data curve", while SDL and εSC are along the loss axis. MNIST table (ε=0.1): SDL 260.6 (CIFAR-pretrained features), 1837.08 (pixels), >1704.77 (VAE); εSC 3395, 31936, >31936. Curves are estimated with 8 bootstrap samples and initializations per point — [arXiv:2009.07368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07368)
- **Lan, Geyer, Chemla, Katzir (2022)**, "Minimum Description Length Recurrent Neural Networks", TACL 10:785–799, doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00489, arXiv:2111.00600. This is an explicit two-part MDL objective $|H|+|D{:}H|$ with weights encoded as signed fractions, optimized by a genetic algorithm rather than backprop. The code is exact, but the method is not a cheap estimator for standard MLPs — [arXiv:2111.00600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00600)
- **Elmoznino et al. (2024/2025)**, "In-context learning and Occam's razor", arXiv:2410.14086 (ICML per Semantic Scholar venue). Prequential code length is "the area under the curve", and "the model's complexity is related to the learner's speed of adaptation". Next-token ICL objectives meta-learn to minimize prequential code length — [arXiv:2410.14086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14086)

#### 0.D Learning-curve based complexity
- **Hestness et al. (2017)**, "Deep Learning Scaling is Predictable, Empirically", arXiv:1712.00409. $\varepsilon(m)\sim\alpha m^{\beta_g}$, with $\beta_g$ "usually… between −0.07 and −0.35" in real applications. "Model improvements only shift the error but do not appear to affect the power-law exponent", and there is a transition from a "best guessing" small-data region to the power-law region — [arXiv:1712.00409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00409)
- **Sharma & Kaplan (2020)**, "A Neural Scaling Law from the Dimension of the Data Manifold", arXiv:2004.10802 (JMLR 2022 [UNVERIFIED]). Predicts exponent $\alpha\approx 4/d$ for data-manifold intrinsic dimension $d$, and tests this in teacher/student setups — [arXiv:2004.10802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10802)
- **Pandey (2024)**, "gzip Predicts Data-dependent Scaling Laws", arXiv:2405.16684. Metric: median over 1000 sampled token sequences of gzip compressed-bytes / raw-bytes. Six PCFG datasets and LMs from 4.4M to 1.4B parameters. As data "becomes less compressible (more complex), the scaling law's compute-optimal frontier gradually increases its preference for dataset size over parameter count" — [arXiv:2405.16684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16684)
- **Hutter (2021)**, "Learning Curve Theory", arXiv:2102.04074. Cited by the epiplexity paper as showing that data properties shape power-law learning curves — [arXiv:2102.04074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04074); [epiplexity related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- **Viering & Loog**, "The Shape of Learning Curves: a Review", IEEE TPAMI 45:7799–7819 (2023), doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3220744, arXiv:2103.10948. The revised version "expresses… more scepticism towards the idea that it has been proven that power laws often provide accurate learning curve models" — [arXiv:2103.10948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10948)

#### 0.E Example-level difficulty / learnability scores
- **RHO-LOSS**: Mindermann, Brauner, Razzak, Sharma, Kirsch, Xu, Höltgen, Gomez, Morisot, Farquhar, Gal, "Prioritized Training on Points that are Learnable, Worth Learning, and Not Yet Learnt", ICML 2022, PMLR 162:15630–15649, arXiv:2206.07137. Selection: $\arg\max_{(x,y)\in B_t} L[y|x;D_t]-L[y|x;D_{ho}]$ (training loss minus "irreducible holdout loss", IL), using Approximation 2 $L[y|x;D_{ho},D_t]\approx L[y|x;D_{ho}]$. The IL model is computed once, "can be small, trained with no holdout data, and reused across target architectures". One ResNet18 IL model trained for 37× fewer steps (62% accuracy) served all 40 seeds of 5 target architectures. The reducible loss "can be negative". High-loss or high-gradient-norm selection picks "overwhelmingly those with noise-corrupted labels" — [arXiv:2206.07137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07137)
- **JEST learnability**: Evans, Parthasarathy, Merzic, Hénaff, "Data curation via joint example selection further accelerates multimodal learning", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.17711. $s_{\rm learn}(B|\theta,\theta^*)=s_{\rm hard}(B|\theta)+s_{\rm easy}(B|\theta^*)=\ell(B|\theta)-\ell(B|\theta^*)$ (batch-level; after Mindermann et al.). The hard-learner score "will also up-sample noisy examples" — [arXiv:2406.17711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17711)
- **EL2N / GraNd**: Paul, Ganguli, Dziugaite, "Deep Learning on a Data Diet: Finding Important Examples Early in Training", NeurIPS 2021 (vol. 34, pp. 20596–20607), arXiv:2107.07075. GraNd $\chi_t(x,y)=\mathbb E_{w_t}\|g_t(x,y)\|_2$. EL2N $=\mathbb E\|p(w_t,x)-y\|_2$, computed "a few epochs into training" and "averaging the scores from ten independent training runs". Removing a small set of the very highest-EL2N examples helps, "enhanced in a corrupted label regime". arXiv v2 carries an erratum: GraNd **at initialization** results were wrong because of a Flax bug — [arXiv:2107.07075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07075)
- Kirsch (2023), "Does 'Deep Learning on a Data Diet' reproduce? Overall yes, but GraNd at Initialization does not", TMLR 2023, arXiv:2303.14753. EL2N at epoch 20 replicates. GraNd at init correlates with input norm and does not beat random pruning — [arXiv:2303.14753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14753)
- **Forgetting events**: Toneva, Sordoni, Tachet des Combes, Trischler, Bengio, Gordon, "An Empirical Study of Example Forgetting during Deep Neural Network Learning", ICLR 2019, arXiv:1812.05159. A forgetting event is a transition from correctly to incorrectly classified between presentations; the score is the event count, and unforgettable examples never have one. Unforgettable examples are "stable across seeds and strongly correlated from one neural architecture to another". "Examples with noisy labels are among the most forgotten examples." Code: github.com/mtoneva/example_forgetting — [arXiv:1812.05159](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05159)
- **Dataset cartography**: Swayamdipta, Schwartz, Lourie, Wang, Hajishirzi, Smith, Choi, EMNLP 2020, pp. 9275–9293, doi:10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.746, arXiv:2009.10795. Confidence = mean over epochs of gold-label probability; variability = its standard deviation across epochs; both from a single run. Regions: easy-to-learn (high confidence, low variability), ambiguous (high variability; "contribute the most towards out-of-distribution generalization"), and hard-to-learn (low confidence, low variability; "many of which we find are mislabeled") — [arXiv:2009.10795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10795)
- **Variance of Gradients (VoG)**: Agarwal, D'souza, Hooker, CVPR 2022 (doi:10.1109/CVPR52688.2022.01012), arXiv:2008.11600. For input-gradient maps $S_t$ at K checkpoints: $\mu=\frac1K\sum_tS_t$, $\mathrm{VoG}_p=\sqrt{\frac1K\sum_t(S_t-\mu)^2}$, $\mathrm{VoG}=\frac1N\sum_p\mathrm{VoG}_p$, then class-normalized. "In practice the last 3 checkpoints are sufficient for a robust VoG ranking." High VoG "over-index[es] on corrupted or memorized examples" — [arXiv:2008.11600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11600)
- **C-score**: Jiang, Zhang, Talwar, Mozer, "Characterizing Structural Regularities of Labeled Data in Overparameterized Models", ICML 2021, PMLR 139:5034–5044, arXiv:2002.03206. Consistency profile $C_{\mathcal P,n}(x,y)=\mathbb E_{D\sim\mathcal P^n}[P(f(x;D\setminus\{(x,y)\})=y)]$; C-score $=\mathbb E_n[\hat C_{\hat D,n}(x,y)]$. Estimated with 2,000 models per subset ratio. The MNIST architecture was an MLP(512,256,10). The score "identifies out-of-distribution and mislabeled examples at one end… and strongly regular examples at the other". The cheap proxy "learning speed has a strong correlation with the C-score" — [arXiv:2002.03206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03206)
- **Area Under the Margin (AUM)**: Pleiss, Zhang, Elenberg, Weinberger, NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2001.10528. AUM is the average over training of (assigned-class logit − largest other logit), with deliberately mislabeled "threshold samples" used to set a cutoff. It is designed to isolate mislabeled data — [arXiv:2001.10528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10528)
- **Memorization / influence**: Feldman & Zhang, "What Neural Networks Memorize and Why", NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2008.03703 (the C-score holdout estimator is "based on the estimator proposed in Feldman & Zhang (2020)"). Koh & Liang, "Understanding Black-box Predictions via Influence Functions", ICML 2017, PMLR 70:1885–1894, arXiv:1703.04730. Pruthi et al., "Estimating Training Data Influence by Tracing Gradient Descent" (TracIn), NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2002.08484. Ilyas et al., "Datamodels", arXiv:2202.00622. Park et al., "TRAK", ICML 2023, PMLR 202:27074–27113, arXiv:2303.14186 — [C-score paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03206); [arXiv:1703.04730](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04730); [arXiv:2002.08484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08484); [arXiv:2202.00622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622); [arXiv:2303.14186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14186)
- Formulas stated from standard knowledge [UNVERIFIED against PDFs in this pass]:
  - Feldman–Zhang memorization: $\mathrm{mem}(\mathcal A,S,i)=\Pr_{h\sim\mathcal A(S)}[h(x_i)=y_i]-\Pr_{h\sim\mathcal A(S\setminus i)}[h(x_i)=y_i]$.
  - Influence: $\mathcal I_{\rm up,loss}(z,z_{\rm test})=-\nabla_\theta L(z_{\rm test},\hat\theta)^\top H_{\hat\theta}^{-1}\nabla_\theta L(z,\hat\theta)$.
  - TracIn self-influence: $\sum_t\eta_t\|\nabla\ell(w_t,z)\|^2$.
- **Perplexity filtering**:
  - Marion, Üstün, Pozzobon, Wang, Fadaee, Hooker, "When Less is More: Investigating Data Pruning for Pretraining LLMs at Scale", arXiv:2309.04564. They compare keeping the bottom, middle or top of the reference-model perplexity distribution. The best variant was "keeping the middle subset using a 52B parameter reference model". Perplexity pruning beat EL2N and memorization pruning, which "achieve similar performances to random pruning despite being far more computationally expensive" — [arXiv:2309.04564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04564)
  - Ankner et al., "Perplexed by Perplexity: Perplexity-Based Data Pruning With Small Reference Models", arXiv:2405.20541 (ICLR 2025 per Semantic Scholar venue). A 125M reference model improves a 3B model "by up to 2.04" average downstream points. The best criterion depends on the corpus: high perplexity on the Pile, medium perplexity on Dolma — [arXiv:2405.20541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20541)
- **Sorscher et al. (2022)**, "Beyond neural scaling laws: beating power law scaling via data pruning", NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2206.14486. Finding: "Keep easy examples when total data is scarce", "keep hard examples when total data is abundant" (confirmed with EL2N on CIFAR-10/ResNet18) — [arXiv:2206.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14486)

#### 0.F V-information and PVI
- **Xu, Zhao, Song, Stewart, Ermon (2020)**, "A Theory of Usable Information Under Computational Constraints", ICLR 2020, arXiv:2002.10689. $H_{\mathcal V}(Y)=\inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\mathbb E[-\log f[\varnothing](Y)]$, $H_{\mathcal V}(Y|X)=\inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\mathbb E[-\log f[X](Y)]$, $I_{\mathcal V}(X\to Y)=H_{\mathcal V}(Y)-H_{\mathcal V}(Y|X)$ — [arXiv:2002.10689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10689); definitions as restated in [arXiv:2110.08420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08420)
- **Ethayarajh, Choi, Swayamdipta (2022)**, "Understanding Dataset Difficulty with V-Usable Information", ICML 2022 (Outstanding Paper), PMLR 162:5988–6008, arXiv:2110.08420. PVI: $\mathrm{PVI}(x\to y)=-\log_2 g[\varnothing](y)+\log_2 g'[x](y)$. Here $g'$ is trained with inputs and $g$ with null inputs. The dataset-level V-information is the mean PVI. "The higher the PVI, the easier the instance". Negative PVI "can happen for many reasons (e.g., mislabelling)". PVI estimates are "highly correlated (Pearson r ≥ 0.75) across different models, seeds, and training time" **on datasets containing more usable information** — [arXiv:2110.08420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08420)

#### 0.G Compression-based measures
- **Delétang et al. (2024)**, "Language Modeling Is Compression", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.10668. Arithmetic coding makes log-loss equal to compressed length. They distinguish the "online setting" (a model trained on the stream being compressed, i.e. prequential) from the "offline" setting with pretrained models. Chinchilla 70B reaches 43.4% on ImageNet patches and 16.4% on LibriSpeech, against PNG 58.5% and FLAC 30.3%, "not accounting for model parameter size". Adjusted compression rates that include parameter cost show that scaling beyond a point hurts compression — [arXiv:2309.10668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668)
- **Huang, Zhang, Shan, He (2024)**, "Compression Represents Intelligence Linearly", COLM 2024, arXiv:2404.09937. $\mathrm{BPC}=\sum_{i=1}^N-\log_2p_{\rm model}(x_i|x_{1:i-1})/T$ (T = characters). Pearson correlation between benchmark score and BPC is about −0.95 — [arXiv:2404.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09937)
- **Yoran, Zheng, Gloeckle, Gehring, Synnaeve, Cohen (2025)**, "The KoLMogorov Test: Compression by Code Generation", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2503.13992. Compression by generating the shortest program that outputs the sequence, on audio, text, DNA and synthetic program outputs. "Both GPT4-O and LLAMA-3.1-405B struggle". 1.5B code models trained on synthetic data beat prompted models by >40% but "struggle on real sequences" — [arXiv:2503.13992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13992)

#### 0.H Intrinsic dimension, effective parameters, weight information
- **Li, Farkhoor, Liu, Yosinski (2018)**, "Measuring the Intrinsic Dimension of Objective Landscapes", ICLR 2018, arXiv:1804.08838. Train in a random subspace $\theta^{(D)}=\theta_0^{(D)}+P\theta^{(d)}$. $d_{\rm int90}$ is the smallest $d$ reaching 90% of baseline performance. Table 1: MNIST FC 750, LeNet 290. Shuffled pixels: FC 750, LeNet 1,400. **Shuffled labels (FC, D=959,610): 190,000**, "3.8 floats per memorized label", measured relative to training accuracy. CIFAR-10: FC 9k, LeNet 2.9k. They interpret $d$ as "an upper bound on the MDL of the problem solution". Blier & Ollivier list intrinsic dimension as an (incomplete) codelength bound, >9.28 kbits on MNIST — [arXiv:1804.08838](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838); [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- **Achille, Paolini, Mbeng, Soatto**, "The Information Complexity of Learning Tasks, their Structure and their Distance", Information and Inference: A Journal of the IMA (doi:10.1093/imaiai/iaaa033), arXiv:1904.03292. Definition 5.1: $C_\beta(D;P,Q)=\mathbb E_{w\sim Q(w|D)}[L_D(p_w(y|x))]+\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(Q(w|D)\|P(w))$, where the KL term is the "information in the parameters". The abstract says the framework "can handle corner cases, such as learning random labels, where other distances such as Kolmogorov's fail" — [arXiv:1904.03292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03292)
- **Achille, Paolini, Soatto (2019)**, "Where is the Information in a Deep Neural Network?", arXiv:1905.12213. It connects measurable quantities "such as the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix" to generalization and invariance via a PAC-Bayes form $\mathbb E_{w\sim Q}[L_D(p_w)]+\frac{\beta}{N}[\mathrm{KL}(Q\|P)+\log\frac1\delta]$ — [arXiv:1905.12213](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12213)
- **Maddox, Benton, Wilson (2020)**, "Rethinking Parameter Counting in Deep Models: Effective Dimensionality Revisited", arXiv:2003.02139. $N_{\rm eff}(A,z)=\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_i+z}$ on Hessian eigenvalues (after MacKay 1992) — [arXiv:2003.02139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02139)
- Related PAC-Bayes and data-complexity entries cited by the epiplexity paper: Dziugaite & Roy, "The size of teachers as a measure of data complexity: PAC-Bayes excess risk bounds and scaling laws", AISTATS 2025. Finzi et al. (2025), "Compute-optimal LLMs provably generalize better with scale", arXiv:2504.15208 — [epiplexity bibliography](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)

### Inferences
- The epiplexity prequential estimate is a rebranding plus refinement of a known quantity: Blier & Ollivier's "information in the trained parameters", Voita & Titov's "model cost", and above all Zhang et al.'s $L_{IT}$. The novelty lies in (i) the time bound and compute-optimal model selection over $(N,D)$, (ii) its interpretation as the structural part of a two-part code, and (iii) the requential code. The review should credit these antecedents explicitly, since 2601.03220 cites only Zhang et al. (2020) and Whitney et al. (2020) among them.
- RHO-LOSS/JEST "learnability" $\ell(x|\theta_t)-\ell(x|\theta^*)$ is, summed over training, a per-example discretization of "loss above the floor". The integral over training time of the reducible loss of the examples being trained on is essentially an example-level prequential-epiplexity integrand. EpiGen's reward $r_t$ (loss reduction on a buffer) is the incremental version. This bridge is inferred and is not stated in any source.

### Gaps
- Exact PMLR page ranges for Perez et al. (ICML 2021), final venues for Ilyas et al. (Datamodels) and Sharma & Kaplan, and the exact venue/year for Elmoznino et al. and Achille et al. "Critical learning periods" (arXiv:1711.08856) were not confirmed. Check them in DBLP; DBLP was rate-limited (HTTP 429) during this pass.
- The Blier & Ollivier NeurIPS page range differs between sources (Semantic Scholar gives 2220–2230). Check against the NeurIPS proceedings page.
- The feasibility of EpiSelect/EpiGen at small scale and its compute details were not extracted, beyond a 124M model mentioned in secondary summaries.

## Q1. Can each method be computed on MNIST / CIFAR-10 / synthetic tasks with an MLP in minutes-to-hours on one Colab GPU, and which choices make it unstable?

### Takeaway
Almost everything is feasible at MNIST/CIFAR-MLP scale on one GPU. Single-pass or replay-based prequential codes, epiplexity's prequential estimate, EL2N, forgetting, cartography, VoG, PVI, RHO-LOSS scores and gzip each cost about one to a few training runs. Block-wise codes with doubling blocks cost about 2× one run per seed and hyperparameter setting. C-score (2,000 models per subset ratio), datamodels and intrinsic dimension for random-label tasks are the expensive outliers. The main sources of instability are:
- the learning rate and schedule (the area under the curve is dominated by early steps and by the final-loss reference);
- small-block overfitting and miscalibration (the "catch-up phenomenon");
- whether the final-loss reference is measured on memorized training data;
- data ordering and seed variance;
- noisy hyperparameter-frontier selection.

### Cited Findings
- Reported compute for prequential MDL with MI/RS: MNIST LeNet 1.4e13 FLOPs, MNIST MnistNet 4.5e14, CIFAR-10 VGG++ 5.9e15, CIFAR-10 WRN-28-10 3.7e16 (the same order as ERM training) — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Blier & Ollivier ran their prequential codes with an MLP (2×256 on MNIST, 2×512 on CIFAR), Adam lr 1e-3, and doubling blocks starting at 8 or 10 examples — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- Catch-up instability: "Large architectures might overfit during the first steps of the prequential encoding… the encoding cost of the first packs of data might be worse than with the uniform code". VGGb needed 5,000 CIFAR samples to reach a cumulative ratio below 1, although per-label cost dropped below uniform after about 1,000. Remedy: switching or self-switching between models (Van Erven et al. 2012) — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- Randomness: prequential MDL "is impacted by random factors such as the order of examples, model initialization, and randomness during training". RDA reports mean ± s.e. over 5 seeds — [Perez et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03872)
- Voita & Titov claim MDL probing results are "more informative and stable than the standard probes", and that conclusions "are not affected by an underlying probe setting" — [arXiv:2003.12298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)
- Whitney et al. estimate each loss-data-curve point from "eight bootstrap-sampled evaluation datasets and initializations". They note VA/MDL rankings flip with evaluation-set size, and that SDL/εSC can only report ">" bounds when the target loss is not reached — [arXiv:2009.07368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07368)
- Epiplexity estimates depend on hyperparameter quality: "The better these hyperparameters are optimized, the more accurate our estimates become". The paper uses μP to stabilize learning-rate transfer and a constant LR with EMA instead of decaying schedules. The empirical Pareto frontier over finitely many runs "is jagged and includes many spurious points", which they fix with a lower convex hull and median point — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Epiplexity's train/test caveat: prequential losses are test-like "because they evaluate the log probabilities on a batch before it is trained on… In cases where train and test diverge, such as when there is overfitting, this difference could become important." — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- The Blier-style difference computed on training data "is heavily affected by model M's degree of overfit". The held-out version ($L_{IT}$) stays near 0 for random labels on CIFAR-10 — [Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07624)
- Requential coding is 2–10× slower than prequential in the epiplexity paper; its overhead depends on "batch size, sequence length, and inference implementation" — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2). The overhead is about 0.33× if teacher checkpoints exist — [arXiv:2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11883v1)
- RHO-LOSS cost: one small IL model, which can be "a small, cheap CNN" and needs no holdout set, then forward passes on candidate batches — [arXiv:2206.07137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07137)
- EL2N needs a few epochs × 10 runs. GraNd at initialization is unreliable (not reproducible; correlates with input norm) — [arXiv:2107.07075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07075); [Kirsch 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14753)
- Forgetting counts are "stable across seeds" and transfer across architectures — [arXiv:1812.05159](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05159)
- VoG is stable with as few as 3 checkpoints — [arXiv:2008.11600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11600)
- Cartography needs a single training run — [arXiv:2009.10795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10795)
- C-score used 2,000 models per subset ratio (MNIST with an MLP(512,256,10)). The learning-speed proxy is cheap and strongly correlated — [arXiv:2002.03206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03206)
- PVI needs two fine-tuned models (with and without input). Seed stability (r ≥ 0.75) was reported for datasets with more usable information — [arXiv:2110.08420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08420)
- Intrinsic dimension: $d_{\rm int90}$ uses "one-run measurements" at each $d$. Beyond MNIST-size networks "it [is] necessary to use more efficient methods of generating and projecting from random subspaces" (sparse/Fastfood). The 90% threshold was chosen so values are "robust to small noise in measured performance" — [arXiv:1804.08838](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838)
- Blier & Ollivier found that variational (Bayesian) codes gave much worse codelengths than prequential codes on MNIST and CIFAR, even for their own objective — [arXiv:1802.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)

### Inferences
- **Colab timing estimates** (my own arithmetic, not from sources). A T4 has about 8 TFLOPS fp32 peak. At about 30% utilization, 1.4e13 FLOPs (MNIST LeNet prequential) takes seconds and 5.9e15 FLOPs (CIFAR-10 VGG++) about 40 minutes. An MNIST/CIFAR MLP is cheaper still. A single-pass prequential/epiplexity estimate with an MLP is therefore a minutes-scale job; 5 seeds × a small LR sweep × 3 dataset variants stays within a few hours. Block-wise codes with doubling blocks train on about $\sum_s t_s\approx 2N$ examples in total, roughly 2× a full run, times the per-block epoch count.
- **Critical pitfall for MNIST/CIFAR (multi-epoch, small data).** If $\log 1/P_M(Z_i)$ in Eq. 8 is evaluated on the *training* examples after multi-epoch training, a memorizing MLP drives it toward 0 on random labels. The "area above final loss" then approaches $n\log K$, a large spurious "epiplexity". Use held-out final loss (Zhang et al.'s $L_{IT}$), strictly single-pass streams, or the last block's online loss as the floor. This is a direct consequence of the Zhang et al. critique and the epiplexity paper's own caveat; it has not been run in this pass.
- **LR sensitivity.** With a too-small LR the curve decays slowly and the area grows, but the final loss may not converge, which biases the floor upward. With a too-large LR, online losses are noisy and can exceed uniform (catch-up). A decaying schedule makes the "final loss" depend on horizon. The epiplexity recipe of a constant LR, an EMA of iterates for evaluation, and LR tuned once on a small model addresses these.
- **Block sizes.** The first block is paid at the uniform rate ($t_1\log_2K$), so a larger $t_1$ inflates the code. Very small first blocks with large models trigger catch-up. Doubling or log-uniform blocks, with calibration and early stopping per block, are the standard compromise.
- **Seeds and ordering.** Report mean ± s.e. over at least 3–5 seeds and data orders (as in Perez et al.). For example-level scores, averaging across roughly 10 runs (as with EL2N) is the norm.

### Gaps
- No source reports wall-clock times on Colab specifically; the estimates above are FLOP-based inference.
- No published systematic study was found of the variance of the epiplexity prequential estimate itself across seeds, LRs and block sizes on MNIST/CIFAR-scale MLPs. This is a genuine gap the review can flag.

## Q2. Which measures capture "learnable structure" (large for structured data, small for both noise and trivial data), and which measure "difficulty" or "noise" (large for random labels)?

### Takeaway
- **Structure-like** (small for both noise and trivial data): area above the final/held-out loss (Blier & Ollivier's parameter information, Voita & Titov's model cost, Zhang et al.'s held-out $L_{IT}$, epiplexity's prequential estimate), the requential code, SDL when ε is set at the irreducible-loss floor, and example-level learnability (RHO-LOSS, JEST, EpiGen reward).
- **Total-information or difficulty measures** (increase with noise): total prequential or online codelength, εSC with a fixed target, gzip and LLM bits-per-character, KoLMogorov program length, EL2N/GraNd, forgetting counts, VoG, AUM, memorization scores, and intrinsic dimension or information-in-weights when measured on memorized training data.
- **Regularity or "ease" measures** that are high for trivial data (not structure amounts): C-score, cartography confidence, PVI.
- **V-information** is about 0 for both random and constant labels, but it saturates at $H(Y)$ for any deterministic rule. It therefore cannot tell a simple rule from a complex one; epiplexity can.

### Cited Findings
- Epiplexity is designed so that uniform noise has trivial epiplexity ("a model as simple as the uniform distribution achieves a small two-part code length, despite having large time-bounded entropy") and simple periodic strings also have low epiplexity — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Raw prequential code length contains the data-entropy term $\sum_tH(X_t)$, which grows linearly with noise. Requential coding removes it ("independent of… data entropy") — [arXiv:2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11883v1)
- Held-out information transfer is about 0 for random labels on CIFAR-10 despite high train accuracy — [Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07624)
- Total online codelength is compared against the uniform code $n\log_2K$ (maximal for random labels). Compression is bounded by mutual information: $H(y)-\mathbb E[L(y|x)]\le I(y;x)$ — [Voita & Titov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)
- Control tasks (random type-level labels) reach similar probe accuracy to linguistic tasks, but only with larger probes. MDL shows this as a larger codelength or model cost — [Voita & Titov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)
- SDL requires $\varepsilon\ge H(Y|X)$. It integrates the surplus loss above ε, and εSC is the sample count to reach ε. When ε is unreachable, only lower bounds (">") can be reported — [Whitney et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07368)
- RHO-LOSS explicitly avoids "redundant and noisy points that are already learnt or not learnable". High-loss and high-gradient-norm selection instead picks "overwhelmingly those with noise-corrupted labels" — [Mindermann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07137)
- JEST: the hard-learner score "will also up-sample noisy examples". Learnability (learner loss − reference loss) up-samples data "both unlearned and learnable" — [Evans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17711)
- EpiGen/EpiSelect: "both trivial and noisy data have near-zero epiplexity" — [arXiv:2608.11746](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11746)
- EL2N: very-high-score examples hurt, especially with corrupted labels — [Paul et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07075)
- Forgetting: noisy labels are among the most forgotten — [Toneva et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05159)
- VoG over-indexes on corrupted and memorized examples — [Agarwal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11600)
- AUM isolates mislabeled data — [Pleiss et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10528)
- Cartography: "hard-to-learn" (low confidence, low variability) examples are frequently mislabeled. "Ambiguous" (high-variability) examples help OOD generalization most — [Swayamdipta et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10795)
- C-score: low for mislabeled and OOD examples, high for "strongly regular" ones — [Jiang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03206)
- PVI: higher means easier; negative PVI arises e.g. from mislabelling — [Ethayarajh et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08420)
- V-information is non-negative and zero under independence — [Xu et al. 2020 via Ethayarajh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08420)
- The epiplexity paper argues V-entropy and pseudoentropy "capture only the random component of information" — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- Intrinsic dimension counts memorization as complexity: shuffled-label MNIST needs $d_{\rm int90}=190{,}000$ (on training accuracy), against 750 for real labels — [Li et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838)
- gzip compressibility rises with syntactic complexity, and less compressible data shifts scaling toward data. The metric itself is compressed/raw size, i.e. an entropy-like proxy — [Pandey 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16684)
- Deep networks fit noise but "learn simple patterns first", with qualitative differences in optimization on noise vs real data (MNIST, CIFAR-10) — [Arpit et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05394). Networks can fit random labels at all — [Zhang et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03530)
- Perplexity pruning works best when keeping the *middle* of the perplexity distribution (Marion et al., 52B reference) — [arXiv:2309.04564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04564). Ankner et al. found high perplexity best on the Pile and medium on Dolma — [arXiv:2405.20541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20541)
- The optimal pruning direction flips with data abundance: keep easy examples when data is scarce, hard examples when it is abundant — [Sorscher et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14486)

### Inferences
- **Classification table** (inferred from the cited behaviours; "noise" = random labels, "trivial" = constant or very simple labels):

| Score | Noise | Trivial | Structured | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total prequential/online codelength | high (≈ n log K) | ≈0 | medium | total info (entropy + structure) |
| Area above held-out final loss (L_IT, epiplexity-preq) | ≈0 | ≈0 | high | **structure** |
| Area above *train* final loss (multi-epoch) | high (memorization) | ≈0 | high | structure + memorized noise |
| Requential code | ≈0 | ≈0 | high | **structure** |
| SDL, ε = irreducible floor | ≈0 | ≈0 | high | structure-like |
| SDL / εSC, fixed ε below noise floor | ∞ / undefined | ≈0 | finite | difficulty |
| Learning-curve exponent | ~0 (flat) | fast saturation | power law | rate, ambiguous |
| gzip / LLM BPC / KT program length | high | low | medium | entropy / K-complexity |
| RHO-LOSS / JEST learnability (per example) | ≈0 or negative | ≈0 once learnt | high while being learnt | **learnable-not-yet-learnt** |
| EL2N, GraNd, forgetting, VoG, AUM, memorization, self-influence | high | low | medium–high | difficulty / noise |
| C-score, cartography confidence, PVI | low | high | high | regularity / ease |
| V-information (dataset) | ≈0 | ≈0 | ≤ H(Y), saturates | predictable info, not rule complexity |
| Intrinsic dim. / info in weights (train fit) | very high | low | medium | capacity used (incl. memorization) |

- Cartography *variability* and "middle-perplexity" selection are heuristic, practice-derived approximations to the "neither trivial nor noise" criterion that epiplexity formalizes. This is an analogy, not a demonstrated equivalence.
- RHO-LOSS is conceptually the closest example-level analogue. Summed over a training run, "training loss − irreducible loss" on the points trained on approximates the area between the learner's loss curve and a floor given by a model trained on other data. It differs from epiplexity in using a separate holdout-trained floor and in being greedy and state-dependent.

### Gaps
- No paper found directly measures epiplexity (or L_IT) together with EL2N, forgetting, RHO-LOSS, PVI and C-score on the same datasets with controlled label noise and controlled rule complexity. That would empirically confirm the table above; it is a clear gap and a natural experiment for the review.
- The claim that information in the weights or effective dimensionality rises for random labels is plausible, and is suggested by Achille et al.'s abstract and by Li et al.'s intrinsic-dimension result. A direct Fisher/N_eff measurement on random labels was not verified in this pass.

## Q3. Practical tricks for cheap, low-variance prequential code-length estimation

### Takeaway
Use mini-batch incremental (online) training with replay instead of retraining from scratch per block, and add forward calibration of the softmax temperature and an EMA of parameters for evaluation. If block-wise coding is used, use doubling or log-uniform blocks with per-block early stopping and calibration. Guard against catch-up with switching or mixture codes (Bayesian model averaging over models or seeds). Average over 3–5 seeds and orders. For epiplexity specifically, use a constant LR plus an EMA of iterates (so one run gives every data budget), μP-style LR transfer, a held-out final loss, and optionally smoothing via scaling-law fits (closed-form area).

### Cited Findings
- Block-wise with retraining from scratch (CI/FS) was the standard: "at some positions t a model is trained from random initialization to convergence on data $D_{<t}$". Mini-batch incremental learning "obtains the lowest description lengths for a wide range of computational budgets". CI/FS won only for very large budgets on CIFAR-100 — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Replay streams implement approximate uniform random replay without large in-memory buffers, using K streams, each with a reset probability. Exponentially decaying replay "lead to significant longer description lengths". Too much replay (K too large) "can be harmful" — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Forward calibration: each new batch is first evaluated, then used for one gradient step on a temperature $\beta$ (with softmax(softplus(β)h)), then used for training. This is "computationally almost free". Block-wise methods use a 90/10 train/calibration split — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- "Generally useful techniques": exponential moving parameter averages (used for next-step evaluation), label smoothing, weight standardization (significant gains for VGG) — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Continual fine-tuning on growing chunks (CI/CF) saves compute. The authors note concerns from research on warm-starting — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931); see "On Warm-Starting Neural Network Training", Ash & Adams, NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:1910.08475 — [arXiv:1910.08475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08475)
- Switch and self-switch codes against catch-up: encode each block with the best of a set of models and pay $L(s)$ for the switch sequence. On CIFAR-10 this saved more than 11 kbits (45.3 → 34.6 kbits); on MNIST the gain was negligible — [Blier & Ollivier 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044)
- Doubling blocks (8, 16, …, 32768 on MNIST) — [Blier & Ollivier 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044). Percentage timesteps 0.1%→100% — [Voita & Titov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298). Nine log-uniform blocks starting at 64, 90/10 split with early stopping per block, 5 seeds with hyperparameters tuned on seed 1 only — [Perez et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03872)
- Temperature calibration "has proven to be surprisingly effective at avoiding overfitting symptoms when training large neural networks on small datasets (Guo et al., 2017; Bornschein et al., 2020)" — [Bornschein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07931)
- Epiplexity recipe: a constant LR with an EMA of iterates (following Hägele et al., NeurIPS 2024, 37:76232–76264) "to avoid separately training a model for intermediate training token budgets". μP/CompleteP LR transfer from a small model. A lower-convex-hull Pareto frontier. Held-out validation loss for $\mathbb E[\log 1/P_M(X)]$. The closed-form area $\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}D_0d^{1-\beta}$ for power-law curves — [arXiv:2601.03220](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03220v2)
- EpiSelect predicts epiplexity gain from scaling-law fits to per-domain training-loss curves instead of using raw noisy curves — [arXiv:2608.11746](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11746)
- Requential stabilizers: EMA teacher and student, teacher smoothing, iso-loss projection. Encoding cost can be traded against code length through block size — [arXiv:2607.11883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11883v1)
- Variational/Bayesian codes are theoretically clean two-part codes whose loss "is exactly the loss function of a variational learning algorithm". Empirically they give much longer codes than prequential codes on deep nets — [Voita & Titov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298); [Blier & Ollivier 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07044). Bits-back and random-coding schemes for weights (MIRACLE) are the explicit-weight alternative: Havasi, Peharz, Hernández-Lobato, "Minimal Random Code Learning", ICLR 2019, arXiv:1810.00440 — [arXiv:1810.00440](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00440)
- Bootstrapping evaluation datasets and initializations (8 per point) to estimate expected loss-data curves — [Whitney et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07368)

### Inferences
- **Ensembling.** Averaging the predictive distributions of M seeds or models at each step is itself a valid prequential code (a mixture code). It costs at most $\log_2 M$ bits more than the best member in hindsight if uniform mixture weights are used, and it typically lowers variance. It is the uniform-mixture special case of Blier & Ollivier's switching idea; the bound is standard MDL, not taken from these sources. The requential paper's finding that ensembles "compress to much smaller sizes" at fixed loss points the same way.
- **Low-variance epiplexity estimate for a Colab MLP (recommended protocol, inferred).** Run a single pass over a stream larger than the eval set, where possible (e.g. MNIST with augmentation or synthetic generators). Record the next-batch (pre-update) loss. Use a constant LR tuned once and an EMA model for evaluation. Take the final floor from held-out loss of the EMA model. Optionally fit $L(D)=E+aD^{-\beta}$ to the online curve and integrate analytically. Average over 3–5 seeds and orders. For multi-epoch settings, use held-out $L_{IT}$ instead of train-set differences.
- **Controls for noise vs structure.** Report both the total prequential codelength and the area above the held-out floor. Include random-label and constant-label controls, which should give ≈0 for the latter measure. The contrast between the two numbers is the empirical signature of epiplexity versus entropy.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how much the epiplexity prequential estimate specifically (as opposed to the total codelength) is reduced in variance by replay, calibration or EMA. Bornschein et al. report total description lengths only.
- The details of the "compute-optimal two-part code" hyperparameter search (Appendix B of 2601.03220) were only partially extracted; its cost at MNIST scale is unreported.
